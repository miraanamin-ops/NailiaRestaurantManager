# Naila (working name)

## What this is
An AI marketing assistant for independent single-site restaurants. The owner runs everything through WhatsApp: the AI drafts the work, the owner taps Approve, Edit or Skip, and nothing goes out unless it passes plain-code safety rules. The user is not a developer, so explain things simply and give exact click paths when you need something from them.

Right now it's being built to test alone with dummy data. No real customers, no live Google connection.

## Stack
Next.js (TypeScript) on Vercel, Supabase (database), GitHub, Claude API (Sonnet), Twilio WhatsApp sandbox (owner channel only), Resend (customer emails from our verified domain mail.dinerai.co.uk, EU region; React Email templates). All keys are Vercel environment variables, never in code.

## Built so far

17. Agent organisation, phase 2 (Reputation): review replies move onto the agent pipeline with `agents/reputation.md`. Shadow mode (the default, per restaurant): every review the hourly check drafts, and every review the owner pastes, also goes to Reputation in the background; its reply goes through the checker loop and is saved next to today's reply for the builder's comparison on /log (Reputation comparison). Live mode (only once the go-live checklist passes: sign-off name, complaint contact, recovery offers, halal and allergen statements): Reputation writes the replies and the checker is blocking (minor fixes applied by code; a fail goes back with the fix notes, at most 2 revisions; then a question). The "needs the owner" question card (food poisoning, allergy, unverifiable claims, fake reviews, staff named, instructions in a review, or the checker can't pass a reply) arrives with Reply publicly / Handle privately / Both: privately closes it, the other two send Reputation back to write the reply with that choice in mind. A "What customers said" section in the Monday report (Reputation's weekly themes, counted by code). Builder controls: Reputation off / shadow / live with the checklist, and a WhatsApp prompt to the owner for missing fields (at most once a day). Checker 0.3 (C7: phone numbers are fine in review replies). Phone is an Identity profile field. No em-dashes in the QR pack, report PDF or report words. `npm run test:agents:stability` runs each live case 3 times and reports verdict flips. `writeReviewReply` stays until every restaurant is live on Reputation and the builder confirms.
18. Agent organisation, phase 3a (Visibility): Google posts, photos and Google updates move onto the agent pipeline with `agents/visibility.md`. Shadow mode (the default): the Monday and Thursday post, every photo the owner sends, every staff photo that isn't a till report, every change to opening hours, special hours or the menu, and the Monday content brief also go to Visibility in the background; its output goes through the checker loop (Google posts with a rendered preview, so the brand checks run) and sits next to today's on /log (one comparison tab per agent: Reputation, Visibility). Photos are checked first: unusable ones get a reason (too dark, blurry, not food, a customer's face or a child, not on the menu...) and, live, a fixed tip to the staff number that sent it. Owner and staff photos go into a photo library (`restaurants.photo_library`, seeded from the Google photos). A change to hours, special hours or the menu becomes an "hours update" or "menu update" draft showing what's on Google now and after, that always needs Approve (never Auto or Draft only, in code); approving logs what would have been sent (Google isn't connected for hours or menus yet). Each Monday, code looks 4 weeks ahead for UK bank holidays (gov.uk, cached) with no special hours set: live, a question in the brief ("Christmas Day is in 3 weeks. Are your hours changing?") with Same hours / Closed / Different hours, which saves the special hours (UNDO-able) and so brings the matching Google update. Live mode needs the go-live checklist (logo, brand colours, hours, emoji setting, 5+ photos) and, for now, TEST_MODE. Reputation now reads opening and special hours (new case: a complaint about being closed on a day the profile says it's closed). Escalation rate (questions as % of reviews, 30 days) on the Reputation comparison. Every agent job records its tokens and estimated cost; Builder controls show cost per restaurant per month by agent; agent prompts are cached. One-off writing stays with today's assistant until the GM router (phase 4).
19. Agent organisation, phase 3b (Insights): the owner's number questions and the Monday report's words move onto the agent pipeline with `agents/insights.md`. First, two Owner profile fields the GM will need: language (default English) and quiet hours (default 11pm to 9am, UK time), on a new "About you" card in settings with the owner's name (`022_owner_preferences.sql`; stored only, nothing uses quiet hours until phase 4). `query_metrics` works out every figure in plain code (sales by day, weekday, hour and item, transactions, average spend, weather, rainy against dry days, reviews, list growth, campaign results, Google stats when connected), each with its period, what it's compared with, its source, a small-sample warning (under 4 weeks of sales data, under 20 redemptions) and days that look wrong (2.5 times or under 0.4 times the usual for that weekday); covers, dish margins and labour cost say what's missing. Insights calls it as a tool (the first agent with a tool loop) and its answer must pass plain-code rules (every figure from a query, every number in the words a figure, "coincided with" not "caused", small samples flagged, an odd till figure asked about first, no staffing, pay, loan or tax advice), then the checker's owner-only checks (facts and C6, decision 3). Shadow (the default): a message that looks like a number question gets today's answer, and Insights answers in the background; each Monday report also gets Insights' headline, sales words and 3 actions around figures code prepared; both sit next to today's words on /log (Insights comparison, with the figures, queries and time taken). Live (test mode only, once there are 14+ days of sales figures from till reports or POS files; a demo's dummy data counts in test mode): Insights answers number questions (today's assistant if it can't) and writes the report's words, with a sales summary on the report page and PDF (today's words if it can't, or if it asks a question). 19 saved cases built on the demo restaurants' dummy year (`agents/tests/insights/`, fixtures from `scripts/export-insights-fixture.mjs`). Insights now reads opening and special hours. Z-report and POS reading stay as they were.
20. Agent organisation, phase 3c (Growth): campaigns move onto the agent pipeline with `agents/growth.md` (email only; WhatsApp stays blocked in the gate). New Commercial profile fields on the settings page: the kinds of offer the owner is happy to run (% off, £ off, a free item with a purchase, kids eat free, 2 for 1) and the birthday offer (`023_growth.sql`). Structured offers: a campaign can carry its offer as fields (`campaigns.offer_details`: type, dishes, the dish it comes with, % or £ value, dates, conditions) and the gate checks the discount cap from them: a free item or £ off as a % of the menu price of the dish it comes with ("with any main" = the cheapest main), 2 for 1 as 50%, blocked if code can't measure it; the wording check stays as a backstop. The owner's own birthday offer and sign-up reward aren't capped, but only when the offer's description is that offer word for word (the gate re-checks it against the restaurant at send time; the agent can't mark anything exempt). A local calendar (`local_calendar`: date, name, type, date uncertain, for every restaurant or one) filled from builder controls, with UK bank holidays from gov.uk. Growth's rules in code: a real segment with its real size, copy that fits the template, an approved kind of offer for dishes on the menu, within the cap, clear dates and conditions, a send time in sending hours on or before the offer's first day, no fake urgency, no targeting by religion, an expected outcome that quotes past results exactly or says "First campaign of this kind, no baseline yet."; the birthday batch uses the birthday offer (or asks); special ideas use menu dishes and never carry a price. Each draft goes through the checker with a picture of the email (brand checks) and a note of what the template adds. Shadow (the default): an owner's ask for an offer gets today's campaign and Growth drafts beside it; the Monday birthday email likewise; each Monday Growth also proposes the week's plan (at most 2, or nothing), drafts for calendar dates 2 to 3 weeks ahead, and suggests up to 2 special ideas (draft only); quiet times are worked out from the last 8 weeks of sales. /log has a Growth comparison with a picture of each email. Live (test mode only, once the checklist passes: cap, offer types, sign-up offer, logo, colours): Growth answers offer requests, writes the birthday batch and Monday drafts (held for the brief) as real campaigns with their offer's fields; today's flow if it can't. Special ideas stay draft only. 18 saved cases.
21. Agent organisation, phase 4 (the GM): the GM (`agents/gm.md`) sits between the owner and the specialists. First, three loose ends: the Agent tests workflow runs only when started by hand; a plain-code price check (every £ amount in a Reputation, Visibility or Growth draft must be a menu price, a value in the offer's fields or the owner's own offers); Growth's send time is used (the gate holds an approved campaign as "scheduled" and the hourly job sends it when due; `024_gm.sql`). A Haiku router reads each owner message and picks one of: reviews, Visibility, one-off writing, Insights, Growth, a fact about the restaurant, a simple profile question, general chat, or "unclear". Fixed commands (APPROVE, SKIP, RUN..., UNDO etc.) stay in code and never reach it. A Sonnet composer writes the GM's words: the brief summary, one short card per item, alerts, replies to simple profile questions (checked by the checker before sending) and the 2-line Monday message; receipts and every number come from code. Rules in code: at most 5 brief cards, urgent first, then by send date, then oldest, the rest roll to tomorrow; at most one alert an hour (a second waits for the next hour) and none in quiet hours (they go when quiet hours end, released by the hourly job, and are dropped if dealt with first); skip offers three one-tap reasons (Wrong tone / Wrong offer / Not now), never a follow-up question; an unclear message gets one question with 2 or 3 buttons; a fact about the restaurant ("we close at 11 on Fridays now") becomes a confirm card with Approve / Edit / Skip that saves through the settings flow (UNDO-able); an "On it, back in a minute" line if a reply takes over 8 seconds, at most once, in the owner's language. Scheduled work's questions (Growth's Monday questions, Insights' missing till reports) arrive in the brief as cards answered in words, and the answer goes back to that agent; Growth's special ideas arrive as Draft-only cards with Like it / Skip. General chat and look-ups stay with today's assistant for now. Shadow (the default): the router runs on every message and a shadow brief on every brief, beside today's on /log (GM comparison, with times). Live (test mode only, once the checklist passes: owner name, language, quiet hours and at least one specialist live): the GM routes, composes the brief, the Monday message and alerts; today's code answers whenever it can't. 40 routing cases and 11 composer cases.
22. TEST_MODE split into separate switches (`src/lib/switches.ts`, pure and unit-tested), so an agent can run live for one restaurant without redirecting every customer's email. EMAIL_REDIRECT: customer emails go to the builder (TEST_EMAIL_ALLOW, builder and owner still get their own); always on in preview and development. TEST_TOOLS: test commands, the test clock, RESET ONBOARDING, new sign-ups as demo restaurants, a test phone moving between demo restaurants; off unless set, and they only ever act on demo restaurants. GOOGLE_MODE: dummy (default) / simulated (reads live Google, posts nothing) / live; preview and development never write to live Google. AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED: true lets each agent the builder set to live (per restaurant, in builder controls) go live once its readiness check passes; false is a kill switch that puts every agent, Reputation included, back in shadow at once without touching the saved modes. Safe by default: missing or unreadable values mean redirect, tools off, Google dummy; an unreadable AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED counts as the kill switch. TEST_MODE is deprecated: it only fills in a switch that isn't set, so TEST_MODE=true behaves exactly as before. Each cron run records its switches (`job_runs.switches`, `supabase/025_job_switches.sql`); /log (builder only) shows the switches now and marks runs where they changed; builder controls show each switch and, per agent, why one set to live runs in shadow; each agent mode or trust level change is named in its audit line. STATUS shows test tools and email redirect.
23. A job queue in Supabase instead of one long hourly run: the hourly job now only queues work (one job per restaurant per task: review check, held GM alerts, feedback emails, sends that are due, weather, and the morning job's first step) and starts workers; workers (`/api/jobs/work`, started every minute by Supabase when jobs are due, by the hourly job and by each WhatsApp message) take jobs a few at a time, each with its own time budget, so one slow or failing restaurant never delays another. Owner messages go on the queue too (answered first, one at a time per restaurant); if one fails the owner gets the usual "Sorry" once and the builder is alerted. The morning job is a chain of steps in the same order as before, the brief last. Retries wait 1, 5, 15 then 60 minutes; a job that uses its tries is dead and the builder is told (never the owner). Limits per job type (Claude, Resend) in the `job_types` table. The old agent queue runs on it (`agent_jobs` stays as the record of agent work). External workers (the coming Python service) use their own login and can only claim, finish or fail their own job types. Alerts for a backed-up queue, stuck jobs and any brief not out by 9:30 (a brief held for WhatsApp's 24-hour rule counts as ready). /log shows each restaurant's queue and dead jobs with a Retry button (in the audit log). Finished jobs are cleared after 14 days, dead ones after 90. A simulated Monday with 50 restaurants has every brief out by 9:13.

26. Analytics service (`analytics/service`, Python), the worker that will run the statistical models outside the app, proven end to end with one benchmark model. It logs in only as `queue_worker` (reads the `analytics` views, adds model outputs, finishes its own runs, uses the `ext_*` queue functions), takes its own job types from the queue, runs each job in its own process with a time budget and heartbeats, finishes or fails it through the queue (retries, backoff, dead-job alerts to the builder), and stops cleanly (the running job is given back without using a try). A model is one file plus one registration line (name, version, kind, role, job type, the views it reads, a run function); the runner creates the `model_runs` row, writes the outputs tied to it and finishes it. First model: `forecast_baseline`, same weekday averaged over the last 4 weeks, 14 days ahead, for orders (all and per channel), covers (all and per channel) and net sales (all), with low/likely/high from the spread. It is a **benchmark** (`model_runs.role`, migration 039): never shown to an owner, whatever its back-test says. Every forecast model scores itself with the same 12-week rolling back-test (issued at 03:05 UK each night, using only trading days that had ended by then); orders/all is the headline. The app queues one job per model per active restaurant at 03:05 UK (09:00 backup), only while `ANALYTICS_JOBS=true`. Dev CLIs: enqueue, run-one, status, check. Hosting: Railway (approved, not deployed yet).

## Design rules to keep following
- Every message to the owner carries a decision or a result. No "just checking in".
- Do the work before asking: arrive with a finished draft so approval is one tap.
- Batch, don't drip. Real-time only for urgent things.
- Nothing reaches a customer unchecked.
- Every feature gets a test command so the owner can trigger it without waiting.

## Now protected
- Only a registered owner number can use WhatsApp, and only for its own restaurant; fake (unsigned or tampered) and duplicate messages are rejected; unknown numbers get one polite reply a day.
- One restaurant can never see or send to another's customers, drafts or reviews (isolation tests).
- Owner web pages need a login; report links expire after 30 days.
- Every send passes the four checks; consent and unsubscribe links are re-checked at send time.
- Every action is logged with who and when; UNDO reverses the owner's last action where possible.
- Scheduled jobs record real outcomes and alert the builder on failure, a stuck run or lateness; no review is skipped by a timeout.
- Test commands and the test clock are off unless TEST_TOOLS is on, and never act on a real (non-demo) restaurant; dummy data can be cleared in one go.
- Claude never receives the customer list or contact details.
- Allergens are never shown to customers, or used in messages to them, until the owner confirms them.
- Customer emails come from our authenticated domain (DKIM, SPF and DMARC pass); nobody gets marketing until they confirm their email; dummy addresses are never emailed; with EMAIL_REDIRECT on (always on in preview and development) nothing reaches real customers.
- The sign-up form has a CAPTCHA (when its keys are set), a bot trap and rate limits; feedback, confirm and delete endpoints are rate-limited.
- Every customer email has Unsubscribe and Delete my data links; the Google review link goes to every customer, never only happy ones.
- Deletions and customer-list exports are in the audit log; export links expire after an hour.
- A WhatsApp number is only linked to a restaurant by sending that restaurant's one-time code (from its logged-in setup page); guessing is capped per number.
- Tests run on every push and before every deploy.
- Staff numbers can only send till reports and sales files for their own restaurant (one restaurant per number); they can't chat, approve or change anything.
- Sales data follows the same isolation rules and tests as everything else; till report photos and POS files are in a private bucket (signed links that last an hour); the AI only sees a few POS rows to work out the columns, with staff, customer and contact details blanked out.
- The AI only reads till reports; plain code decides whether they add up, and nothing doubtful is saved without a YES.
- A question for the owner (about a review, or a bank holiday's hours) is never sent by the gate, and APPROVE ALL leaves it for the owner's own answer.
- Changes to hours or the menu on Google always need the owner's Approve (never Auto or Draft only). No agent goes live unless AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED allows it and its readiness check passes, and AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED=false puts every agent back in shadow at once. Visibility's staff tips are fixed wording. Reputation can only go live once its go-live checklist passes, and then the checker is blocking for its replies.
- Trust levels are per task and only the builder can change them; campaigns, offers, hours, menu and profile changes can never be Auto, and Auto needs a live checker pass on that exact version (enforced in code, not in a prompt).
- With GOOGLE_MODE=simulated (and always in preview and development) the gate itself stops anything reaching live Google; WhatsApp to customers is off until Meta verification.
- No customer gets more than one campaign in any 7 days (birthday emails excepted), checked in code at send time.
- Agents only get the tools listed in their file (an allow-list in code), never a tool that sends, publishes or spends, and only their own restaurant's data. An agent can't change a settings-only profile field (the cap, the send window).
- Scheduled work runs one job per restaurant per task on a queue: one slow or failing restaurant never delays another; two workers never run the same job; a job that keeps failing stops after its tries and the builder is told (never the owner); an owner whose message failed gets one "Sorry". The queue's functions can't be called with the public key, and the external worker login can only touch its own job types, never a table.
- The menu has one home (dishes): `restaurants.menu` is only a copy and the database refuses any other change to it (after 037). Prices, pay rates, costs and targets are never overwritten, and back-tests can only see what was known at the time.
- Every operations table has Row Level Security policies (an owner reaches only their restaurant's rows; history can't be changed through them). The Python service reads only the analytics views (no staff names, photos or contact details) and can only add model outputs and finish its own runs.
- Benchmark model runs (e.g. the baseline forecast) never reach an owner: the app only shows outputs of production runs that succeeded and passed, one file reads model outputs (enforced by a test), and the database hides benchmark runs and their outputs from owner logins. The analytics service refuses production unless told explicitly, never uses the Supabase secret key, and a job is never run by two workers or left locked.
- Staff data is minimal (no NI numbers, addresses, bank details or dates of birth); a person can be forgotten (details gone, wage records kept unnamed).

Drafts from before the agents have no sources of their own, so code lists the profile fields each kind may rely on. Rendered previews exist for emails only (B1 to B4 are skipped for posts and replies). Only the profile fields Reputation, Visibility and the checker need are stored so far. Visibility (phase 3a): Google isn't connected for hours, menus or posts (dummy mode logs what would have been sent); live mode needs AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED=true (or the deprecated TEST_MODE); trend ideas and local calendar dates (religious dates, local events) wait for a trend scan and a local calendar; special hours can only be set by answering the bank holiday question (no settings field yet); a hours update can still be "edited" as text, which doesn't change the hours themselves.
- Insights (phase 3b): unless the GM is live, number questions are spotted by a word list, so some are missed and a few others are sent to it (shadow only costs money). Live mode needs AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED=true (or the deprecated TEST_MODE). Covers aren't recorded (transactions instead); dish margins, labour cost and sales targets need data no restaurant has yet (menu engineering and rival watch are later). Google views, calls and direction requests don't exist until Google is connected. "Last week" is always the last full Monday to Sunday, so RUN REPORT mid-week covers a different week from today's report. When a week's figures are incomplete, Insights asks for the missing till reports rather than writing the report words (live: today's words are kept; the question goes in the brief when the GM is live, otherwise it's only recorded). Answers take 10 to 40 seconds (tool calls plus the checker); the "On it" line covers the wait only when the GM is live.
- Switches (step 22) are Vercel environment variables: a change only takes effect after a redeploy (Vercel, Deployments, the latest one, Redeploy; about two minutes), so the kill switch is fast but not instant. Changing them in Vercel isn't in the restaurant's audit log; the next cron run records them and /log marks the change. Per-restaurant control is the builder's agent mode in settings; the switches are the same for every restaurant.
- Growth (phase 3c): live mode needs AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED=true (or the deprecated TEST_MODE). A scheduled campaign goes out at the first hourly run after its send time (up to an hour late). Questions from the live Monday work, and special ideas, reach the owner only through the GM's brief (when the GM is live); otherwise they're recorded, and special ideas stay on /log.
- The GM (phase 4): live mode needs AGENTS_LIVE_ALLOWED=true (or the deprecated TEST_MODE). General chat, look-ups, pasted reviews and drafts for a specialist in shadow still go to today's single assistant (its big prompt is retired only once the builder confirms). If the router is slow (over 10 seconds) or fails, today's assistant answers. Button titles set per message (the "which did you mean?" choices) haven't been tried on the real Twilio sandbox yet; typing 1, 2 or 3 always works. Allergen and halal statements can't be changed by message (settings page only). Alerts are held at most until the next hourly job, so a held alert can arrive up to an hour after it's due. Replies to simple profile questions go through the checker, which adds a few seconds. The calendar has bank holidays only until the builder adds religious dates, school holidays and local events. Free-item offers are measured against one dish's price, so "free dessert with any main" counts against the cheapest main; owners who want generous free items can raise their cap. Growth asks when details are missing (uncertain dates, reward terms, hours that don't fit), which is right but means fewer drafts until the profile is fuller.
- Job queue (step 23): an owner's reply waits for a worker to start (about half a second; if the kick fails, up to a minute for Supabase's schedule). Owner messages get one try only (a retry could answer twice). Claims are one at a time across the whole queue (a short lock, milliseconds each), fine for hundreds of restaurants, not for many thousands of jobs a minute. Limits are per job type, not one shared Claude limit: about 30 jobs can run at once with today's numbers (the simulation's peak was 33 with owner messages). Local servers share the queue with the live site: a local message is normally answered by the local server (its kick is instant), but the live site's every-minute workers can take queued work too. The Monday post step runs Visibility's job straight away only if an agent slot is free (6 at once); otherwise the post arrives after the brief. PGlite can't test truly simultaneous claims (`scripts/queue-race.mjs` does, on the real database). The simulation's timings use stand-in durations for Claude (10 to 60 seconds) and email, not measured ones.
- Operations data (step 24) is schema only: nothing fills staff, rotas, invoices, recipes, covers, channels, the calendar extensions, prayer times or model outputs yet, and no screen or WhatsApp flow uses them. Menu readers still read the `restaurants.menu` copy (identical to before); a future-dated price change shows in the copy at once. Dishes are matched by name when a menu arrives without ids (onboarding, UNDO, Revert): two dishes with the same name in one restaurant are told apart by position only. The app still bypasses RLS (secret key); the new policies only protect logins that don't. Staff retention (6 years for hours and pay, 3 months for availability) is a proposal to check with an accountant, and `apply_staff_retention` isn't scheduled yet. Weather forecasts are only kept from the day this step went live (Open-Meteo has no issue time, so `issued_at` is when we fetched it). Supabase's login roles: `queue_worker` still has no password until the Python service exists.
- Analytics service (step 26): not deployed yet (Railway approved; ANALYTICS_JOBS stays off until it is). Only the baseline benchmark exists, so nothing is shown to owners. The nightly run at 03:05 UK can't use yesterday (a trading day only counts once it ended at the 05:00 cut-off), so its freshest data is two days old; a run after 05:00 would gain a day. `finish_model_run` lets queue_worker finish any running run, not only its own. Sales per channel include VAT while daily sales are net, so channel sales aren't forecast. Covers forecasts are weak for small groups (the late takeaway's dine-in).
